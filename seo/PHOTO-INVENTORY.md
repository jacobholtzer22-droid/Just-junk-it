## 3. Usability flags

### Recommend excluding, or cropping before use

**`IMG_7034` — legible shipping label.** In the lower-right of the junk pile there is a
white carrier label on a box with a scannable barcode, a routing block, and address text.
At the native 4032 px it is partly readable. Publishing a 1600 px WebP of this frame risks
putting a fragment of a real person's name or address on a public page.
Two ways forward, your pick:
- **(a) Crop the bottom ~20% of the frame.** This removes the label cluster *and* the
  fingertip below, and the remaining image is still a wall of junk that reads perfectly.
  This is what I would do.
- **(b) Drop it.** Costs you the before half of PAIR 03.

**`IMG_7034` — fingertip over the lens**, bottom-left corner. Cosmetic only. Fixed by the
same crop as above.

### Present but, in my read, fine to publish — your call

- **`IMG_0211`** — a worker's bare forearm, jeans and a work boot on the stairs. No face, no
  identifying features. Reads as a real crew mid-job, which helps rather than hurts.
- **`IMG_0241`** — a worker in a black t-shirt crouched behind the washer, head down and
  obscured by the appliance. No face visible.
- **`IMG_7286`** — a bicycle wheel intrudes at the very bottom-left edge. Cosmetic; croppable
  if it bothers you.

### Checked and clean

- **No license plates anywhere.** I specifically cropped and examined the truck in
  `IMG_0209` at native resolution: it is a mud-covered dark crew-cab pickup with a black
  flatbed dump body and mesh sides, shot from the driver's side. No plate is in frame.
- **No house numbers, no mail, no signage naming a property, no customer faces** in any of

- **`IMG_0209` is quietly one of the most valuable files here** — it is the only shot of

## 5. Processing notes for Phase 1

- **Four files are stored rotated and must be rotated 90° during conversion**, or they ship
  sideways: `IMG_7034`, `IMG_7101`, `IMG_7278`, `IMG_7286`. The macOS EXIF orientation flag
  is inconsistent across this set and cannot be trusted — I confirmed correct orientation by
  eye on every file. `IMG_0212` and `IMG_0214` are genuinely portrait and are already correct.
- Mixed aspect ratios: 4 landscape, 6 portrait. The gallery and the service-page photo bands
  have to handle both without cropping heads off a room. Portrait frames are actually an
  advantage on a 390 px screen.
- `sharp` is **not** in the reference repo's `package.json` — that project pre-generated its
  WebP files outside the build. I will add `sharp` as a devDependency here and keep the
  conversion in a one-shot script, so nothing extra ships in the runtime bundle.
